📋  Mentor Guide — How to Use This Module

Leader & Facilitator Notes

Module 2 addresses one of the most powerful and often neglected arenas of Kingdom life: the family. For young adults aged 18–25, this topic is simultaneously urgent and layered with complexity. Some participants are raising siblings. Some carry wounds from difficult homes. Some are newly married. All of them are forming habits right now that will shape their households for the next generation.

This guide is designed for use by any mentor, cell leader, pastor, or discipleship facilitator — authored by Pst. Daniel Thuo. The content and principles apply universally across church contexts.

Part 1  ·  Joshua's Declaration — A Kingdom Choice

A Kingdom Covenant

"As for me and my household, we will serve the LORD."
Joshua 24:15 (NKJV)

Joshua was not a young idealist when he spoke these words. He was an old general who had seen the worst of human nature — slavery, wilderness wandering, warfare, and the death of an entire generation. He had watched leaders crumble. He had buried friends who started well and finished badly. And when the time came for his final address to the nation he had led for decades, he chose one topic above all others:

Household. Family. The people closest to him.

Notice the structure of Joshua 24:15: "Choose for yourselves this day whom you will serve." The phrase "this day" reveals something critical — it is a daily decision, not a one-time declaration. Joshua was not announcing a past achievement. He was making a present, active, repeated choice. And he was anchoring it not in his own willpower but in a covenant relationship with the living God.

This is the spirit of Module 2: not a sentimental celebration of family, but a Kingdom covenant — a deliberate, daily choice about how you will lead the people God has placed closest to you.

What 'Family' Means in This Room

Before we go further — who is in your household right now? At 18 to 25 years old, the answer varies enormously. Some live with parents. Some are caring for younger siblings. Some are far from home for the first time. Some have chosen a faith community as their primary family because their biological one is fractured or absent.

The biblical word for what Joshua chose is household — broader than the nuclear unit, inclusive of all those within your sphere of relational responsibility. This module speaks to all of that.

Opening Discussion  ·  10 min — Pairs, then full group
1

Who is in your 'household' right now — the people you feel genuinely responsible for or deeply connected to?

2

What does it mean to 'serve the LORD' in that context — not just personally, but together with those people?

3

Who first modelled to you what it looks like to lead a household with faith? What did they do?

Part 2  ·  The Word 'Cherish' — A Kingdom Standard

Why Cherish and Not Just Love?

"Love" is true but very broad — we can say we love God, and in the same breath say we love our favourite meal. "Value" is correct, but it can stay in the mind without showing up in how we treat people. "Cherish" is different. It is love that shows up in action.

To cherish someone means you treat them as precious. You protect them, you pay attention to them, and you carry them in your heart even when they are not physically in front of you. A person who is cherished does not have to guess how much they matter — they can feel it. In the way you speak, in how you show up, in whether you remember, in whether you care when nothing is "wrong."

In today's world, where relationships are often fast, shallow, and sometimes transactional, cherishing stands out. It refuses the idea that people are only important when they are useful, visible, or convenient. Instead it says: you are not a burden in my life — you matter to me, even when it costs me something.

Three Dimensions of Cherishing

Active Protection

Intentional, attentive care for another's wellbeing
Following up when a sibling goes silent or withdrawn. Checking on a parent under pressure. Speaking up for someone who cannot defend themselves. Refusing to ignore signs of struggle in people close to you.

Holding Dear

Treating someone as precious, not taken for granted
Prioritising time with family over convenience. Speaking with honour about family members, especially in their absence. Choosing respect and gratitude instead of criticism or neglect.

Longing Tenderness

Carrying people in your heart even across distance and time
Sending voice notes or messages when away. Remembering key dates, milestones, and personal struggles. Maintaining emotional presence even when physically distant.
Part 3  ·  Four Kingdom Ways to Cherish Family

Practical Kingdom Commitments

1

Understand God's Priority Order

"If anyone does not know how to manage his own family, how can he take care of God's church?"
1 Timothy 3:4–5 (NKJV)
1
God
2
Family
3
Ministry

The biblical order is clear. This is not a productivity ranking, but a statement about responsibility and accountability. At the end of life, God will not only ask about public influence or visible success. He will also ask how you loved, led, and stewarded the people He placed closest to you.

There is a subtle but dangerous lie that circulates in some church cultures: that sacrificing family for ministry is a mark of devotion. Paul confronts this directly in 1 Timothy 3. The very first qualification for church leadership is household management. Your private faithfulness precedes your public calling.

"God first" does not mean "ministry first." Ministry is what you do for God. Family is what you steward for God. They are not the same thing — and if they were in competition, Scripture is clear about which one holds priority.
2

Prepare Your Family for Life

"By wisdom a house is built, and through understanding it is established; through knowledge its rooms are filled with rare and beautiful treasures."
Proverbs 24:3–4 (NKJV)

As young adults, you carry something your family members may not have: education, exposure, faith community, and the experience of being discipled. This is not for you alone — it is to be shared.

Deuteronomy 6:7 instructs God's people to teach His commands "when you sit at home and when you walk along the road, when you lie down and when you get up." The setting is not a classroom — it is the ordinary moments of shared life. A meal. A journey. A late-night conversation. The most powerful discipleship rarely happens on a stage.

✦  Practical Question

What wisdom are you carrying that your family needs? What has God taught you through your faith community, this curriculum, or your own walk with Him — that a younger sibling, a cousin, or a parent needs to hear?

3

Protect Your Family

"If one falls down, his friend can help him up. But pity the man who falls and has no one to help him up!"
Ecclesiastes 4:10 (NKJV)

Protecting family does not mean preventing difficulty. Every family will face storms — illness, financial collapse, conflict, grief, failure. The cherishing leader does not promise to remove the storm. They promise to stand in it with you.

Notice three things a cherishing protector offers in the storm:

  • Practical help — showing up with hands and resources
  • Wisdom — bringing God's perspective into a chaotic moment
  • Steady presence — being a non-anxious anchor when everything else is shaking

Protection also includes loving correction — not driven by pride or irritation, but spoken in love, with humility and care. When correction is combined with love, presence, and wisdom, it becomes one of the most protective gifts you can give. It does not control people — it guards their future.

4

Point Your Family to God

"Yet indeed I also count all things loss for the excellence of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord…"
Philippians 3:8 (NKJV)

Of all the ways we cherish our families, this one carries the deepest and most eternal weight. Material support matters. Words of encouragement matter. Emotional presence matters. But all of these remain temporary. Pointing your family to God is different — it is the only kind of impact that outlives this life.

This does not require a pulpit, a title, or theological expertise. It begins with a life that quietly but clearly communicates: God is real, God is faithful, and God is worth trusting. People may not always respond to what you say, but they are deeply shaped by what they consistently see.

Often, the most influential "sermon" your family will ever receive is not a formal message or invitation to church. It is the way you respond when life becomes difficult. How you handle pressure, disappointment, loss, or success can quietly point people to God more powerfully than words alone ever could.

Part 4  ·  When Family Is Complicated

Honest Ground

This session would not be honest if it only described family in beautiful or ideal ways. For many participants, family is complicated. It carries real pain — abandonment, ongoing conflict, emotional distance, abuse, loss, or the pressure of being the one everyone depends on in a household that is not stable. This is not a small issue or a rare experience. For many young adults, this is reality.

In light of what God is doing in your life — how are you choosing to respond to your family moving forward?

Forgiveness and Reconciliation Are Not the Same

Forgiveness

Forgiveness happens inside you. It is choosing to release the weight of what someone did — not because it was okay, not because it didn't hurt, but because you refuse to let it keep controlling your heart. It is rooted in the truth that God has forgiven us far more than we could ever repay.

"Be kind and compassionate to one another, forgiving each other, just as in Christ God forgave you."
Ephesians 4:32 (NKJV)

Reconciliation

Reconciliation is different. It is about rebuilding a relationship. That takes time, safety, trust, and real change — and sometimes it may not be immediately or fully possible. You can forgive someone and still keep boundaries. You can forgive a parent and still protect your emotional health.

"If it is possible, as far as it depends on you, live at peace with everyone."
Romans 12:18 (NKJV)
✦  Facilitator Note

If family-related wounds are surfacing for any participant during this session: this is a holy moment. Encourage them to speak to a cell leader, pastor, or trusted mentor. God is not afraid of pain around family — He is in the business of bringing deep healing there.

"The LORD is near to those who have a broken heart, and saves such as have a contrite spirit."
Psalm 34:18 (NKJV)

The Generational Covenant

Joshua's declaration — 'As for me and my household, we will serve the LORD' — was spoken by a man who grew up as a slave under Pharaoh. His formation happened in the most brutal of conditions. He had every reason to repeat a pattern of powerlessness and fear. Instead, he made a Kingdom covenant to do things differently.

You are not your parents' failures. You are not the wounds of your household. You are a young Kingdom citizen, standing at the beginning of your household leadership — and you have the power, through Christ, to start a new story.

"Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation; old things have passed away; behold, all things have become new."

2 Corinthians 5:17 (NKJV)

"I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit within you…"
Ezekiel 36:26 (NKJV)
Small Group Discussion  ·  Groups of 3–4  ·  12–15 min

Discussion Questions

Choose a facilitator and timekeeper within your group. Work through as many questions as time allows. Be honest — this is a safe space.

Group Questions
1

Cherishing in Practice

Which of the four ways to cherish family feels most natural to you right now? Which one feels most difficult or most neglected in your current season of life?

2

Family and Ministry Balance

Where is the line between prioritising family and neglecting ministry — and where is the line between prioritising ministry and neglecting family? Have you ever seen either extreme in someone's life? What were the consequences?

3

Re-engaging Relationships

Is there a family member you have been taking for granted — someone you have stopped intentionally cherishing? What practical step could help you re-engage that relationship in a healthy way?

4

Public Commitment and Accountability

Joshua made his declaration in public — "as for me and my household." Why is it helpful to share your family commitments with other people who can encourage you and hold you accountable?

Personal Reflection  ·  Set aside 8–10 minutes in quiet

Written Reflection

Take your time. Write honestly. This is between you and God.

Is there a family member you need to forgive? Be honest with yourself. Is there confusion in your heart between forgiving and reconciling?
Write your own version of Joshua 24:15 — specific to your household, your current season, and your covenant with God.
What is one practical action you will take this week to cherish your family more intentionally?
