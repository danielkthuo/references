Introduction

Purpose, Vision & How to Use

This curriculum is designed for mentors, cell group facilitators, and discipleship leaders working with young people — whether in high school, university, post-graduation, or any season of early faith formation. It is equally suited for one-on-one mentoring relationships, small groups, cell churches, campus fellowships, youth ministries, and peer discipleship circles.

The Goal

To raise disciples who are rooted in Scripture, shaped by the Holy Spirit, and actively multiplying the Kingdom in their schools, workplaces, and communities.


The Method

Relational, story-based, and participatory — inspired by Jesus' own model: teaching through everyday life, honest conversation, and shared mission. The curriculum does not require a professional teacher. Any mentor who is living what they teach can use it.

Contextual Principles

Use stories, proverbs, and illustrations from the culture you are serving. Anchor biblical truth in what your group already knows and values. Acknowledge the spiritual worldview already present in your context — fears, syncretism, cultural pressures — and address them biblically and respectfully.

Build relationships first — people share truth within trust. Discipleship without relationship is just instruction.

Session Structure — 90 Minutes

Module 01 · Weeks 1–3 · 2 Sessions

Who Am I? Identity in Christ

Confronting false identity and building on the truth of Scripture
"Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation; old things have passed away; behold, all things have become new." — 2 Corinthians 5:17 NKJV

One of the most urgent discipleship needs for young people in any culture is a settled sense of identity. Students, graduates, and new believers in every context wrestle with the question: "Who am I now?" Many define themselves by academic performance, social standing, family status, or social media persona. This module dismantles those false foundations and builds identity firmly on what God says — loved, forgiven, chosen, and sent.

Session 1: The Identity Crisis of Our Generation

Who does the world say I am? Unpacking the labels we carry — background, performance, comparison, shame. This session challenges all such distortions and grounds identity in God's declaration.

Session 2: Breaking False Identities

The Gospel declares: in Christ, the old is gone and the new has come (2 Corinthians 5:17). No label, no family story, no past sin defines you more than the grace of God.

Renewing the Mind — Romans 12:1–2
  • Identifying the lies you have believed about yourself
  • Replacing them with the truth of Scripture through consistent meditation
  • Allowing trusted community to speak truth into your life
Mentor Note Consider the specific identity pressures your disciples face: academic performance, peer comparison, family expectations, ethnic or social labels, past failures. Encourage honest conversation about where group members have been finding their sense of worth.

Key Activities

  • Scripture Memory: Psalm 139:13–14
  • Mirror Exercise: Write three negative labels you've believed. Find a Bible verse contradicting each. Read them aloud every morning for one week.
  • Application: Send one encouraging message to someone reflecting their God-given identity — not their performance.

Module 02 · Weeks 4–6 · 3 Sessions

The Word: Knowing and Living Scripture

From Bible trivia to life-transforming encounter with God's living Word
"Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path." — Psalm 119:105 NKJV

Disciples often know Bible stories but don't know how to read the Bible for themselves. This module introduces the SOAP method of personal Bible study, addresses common intellectual objections to Scripture, and makes God's Word feel alive and personally relevant.

Session 1: Why the Bible Matters for Your Generation

In an age of social media theology, quick-fix spirituality, and competing worldviews, young people need the skill of discernment more than ever. The Bible is the living Word of God (Hebrews 4:12) that speaks to every dimension of life: relationships, ambition, suffering, sexuality, and purpose.

Canon Criteria — Why These Books?
  • Apostolicity: Written by an apostle or close associate — tracing back to eyewitness testimony
  • Consistency: Teaching agrees with the established apostolic witness; contradictory doctrine was rejected
  • Universal Acceptance: Widely used and accepted across different regional churches
  • Spiritual Power: Evidence of the Holy Spirit's work in readers' lives
The SOAP Method for Daily Bible Study
S
Scripture
Read the passage slowly — even twice. Write it out in your own words.
O
Observation
What do you notice? Who is speaking? What words stand out?
A
Application
How does this apply to your life TODAY? Be specific and concrete.
P
Prayer
Turn your observation and application into a prayer. Speak to God.

Key Activities

  • SOAP Practice: Group reads John 15:1–17 together and each person completes a SOAP journal entry. Pairs share observations.
  • Scripture Memory: 2 Timothy 3:16–17. Memorize in pairs, test each other next session.
  • 4-Week Reading Plan: Mark · Psalms & Proverbs · Ephesians, Colossians, Philippians · James, 1 Peter, 1 John

Module 03 · Weeks 7–9 · 3 Sessions

Prayer as Conversation

Moving from ritual to relationship — discovering prayer as honest, powerful communion with God
"Be anxious for nothing, but in everything by prayer and supplication, with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God." — Philippians 4:6 NKJV

Prayer is often treated as performance — long, loud, or formulaic. This module invites disciples into genuine conversation with God. It addresses common blocks: unanswered prayer, doubt, distraction, and the fear of praying out loud.

Session Breakdown
  • Session 1: What is prayer? Is God really listening? Reading Luke 11:1–13. Jesus' model — simple, relational, persistent.
  • Session 2: Models of prayer — ACTS (Adoration, Confession, Thanksgiving, Supplication). Praying the Psalms. Praying in your own voice, not a "performance voice."
  • Session 3: Corporate prayer and intercession. Praying for your community, for others. Introduction to prayer journaling.
Mentor Note Acknowledge prayer cultures in your context — all-night prayers, fasting traditions, formulaic language. Help disciples distinguish performance from authentic intimacy. Ground all prayer practice in the loving relationship Jesus modelled.

Key Activities

  • Lament Practice: Read Psalm 22 together. Disciples write their own honest lament prayer — real struggles, no pretending.
  • Prayer Walk: Walk your meeting space or neighborhood in small groups, praying specifically for the people and places around you.
  • 30-Day Prayer Partner: Each pair commits to praying for 5 minutes together every day for 30 days.

Module 04 · Weeks 10–12 · 3 Sessions

The Holy Spirit — Your Daily Guide

Understanding who the Spirit is, how He moves, and how to yield to His leading in daily life
"However, when He, the Spirit of truth, has come, He will guide you into all truth; for He will not speak on His own authority, but whatever He hears He will speak; and He will tell you things to come." — John 16:13 NKJV

The Holy Spirit is often either over-emphasized (leading to spiritual excess) or under-emphasized (producing purely intellectual Christianity). This module presents a balanced, biblically grounded picture of the Spirit as guide, comforter, convicter, and empowerer.

Biblical Identity of the Holy Spirit
  • He teaches (John 14:26)
  • He speaks (Acts 13:2)
  • He guides (John 16:13)
  • He can be grieved (Ephesians 4:30)
  • He empowers believers (Acts 1:8)

Discernment: The Three-Source Framework

✦
The Spirit
Aligns with Scripture · Produces love and peace · Points to Christ · Open to community testing
?
My Own Mind
Serves my comfort · Confirmed by my feelings · Stronger when I haven't prayed
!
Another Source
Produces shame or fear · Contradicts Scripture · Bypasses community testing
Four Practical Tests
  • Scripture test — does this align with or contradict the Word?
  • Character test — does following this produce fruit of the Spirit or works of the flesh over time?
  • Community test — do mature, trusted believers affirm this direction? (Prov 11:14)
  • Conviction vs. condemnation — the Spirit convicts toward restoration and hope (Rom 8:1). False sources produce paralyzing shame or grandiosity.

Relationships, Community & the Body of Christ

Friendship, accountability, mentorship, and what it means to truly "do life together"
"As iron sharpens iron, so a man sharpens the countenance of his friend." — Proverbs 27:17 NKJV

God made humans for community. Yet the prevailing culture increasingly isolates young people behind screens or divides them by program, background, or economic class. This module redeems community and helps disciples build authentic, Christ-centred relationships.

Session 1: God Is Community — The Trinity

Before God created a single human being, community already existed. "Let us make mankind in our image" (Genesis 1:26) reveals the Trinity as the original community of self-giving love. We were not made for isolation; we were made in the image of a God who exists in eternal, joyful relationship.

Four Marks of Healthy Accountability

  • Specificity: Ask specific questions — "Did you pray this week? What happened?" produces honest answers.
  • Regularity: Accountability works when it is a rhythm, not a crisis response.
  • Grace, not condemnation: When someone confesses a failure, the right response is prayer, not lecture.
  • Confidentiality: What is shared in the accountability relationship stays there.

Key Activities

  • Relationship Audit: Disciples map their 5 closest relationships. "Does this relationship make me more like Christ?"
  • Cross-group Gathering: Deliberately mix participants across backgrounds for a shared meal or activity.
  • Community Covenant: Each group writes a short covenant — how we will treat one another, pray for one another, and hold each other accountable.

Module 06 · Weeks 16–18 · 3 Sessions

Faith, Culture & Integrity

Navigating pressure, corruption culture, and the call to be salt and light in your institution
"You are the light of the world. A city that is set on a hill cannot be hidden." — Matthew 5:14 NKJV

The moral landscape of any academic or professional environment presents real complexity: exam cheating, plagiarism, harassment, compromised systems. This module calls disciples to the costly but powerful path of integrity.

Session Breakdown
  • Session 1: Holiness is not just religion — it is counter-cultural love. Jesus and the Pharisees: external rules versus transformed hearts.
  • Session 2: Academic and professional integrity as a Gospel issue. Daniel in Babylon as the model. Case studies from your specific context.
  • Session 3: Digital life and witness. How our online behaviour shapes our testimony. Discipleship for the social media generation.

Key Activities

  • Case Study Discussion: "Someone offers you an unfair advantage. What do you do and why?" Roleplay responses.
  • Media Audit: Disciples spend 3 days tracking their screen time and content. "What is shaping my mind more — the Word or my feed?"
  • Integrity Pledge: Each disciple writes and signs a personal integrity commitment specific to their context. Witnessed by their accountability partner.

Navigating Temptation & Moral Purity

Honest, grace-filled guidance on sexual integrity and the daily fight for holiness
"No temptation has overtaken you except such as is common to man; but God is faithful, who will not allow you to be tempted beyond what you are able, but with the temptation will also make the way of escape, that you may be able to bear it." — 1 Corinthians 10:13 NKJV

Four Practical Strategies

1
Accountability
A person who knows your specific struggles and has permission to ask hard questions.
2
Digital Boundaries
Content filters, screen time limits, devices out of the bedroom.
3
The Joseph Principle
Genesis 39: don't debate in place — flee. Interrupt the pattern earlier.
4
Community
Isolation is the enemy of purity. Embed in community.

When You Fall: The Path of Restoration

  • Confess to God — 1 John 1:9. The promise is unconditional.
  • Confess to a trusted person — James 5:16. Bringing sin into the light removes its power.
  • Do not add self-condemnation — Romans 8:1. There is no condemnation for those in Christ.
  • Return to the rhythm immediately — do not allow one failure to produce a spiral.
Mentor Note End with concrete, actionable steps — not vague resolve. Close with the gospel: falling is not the final word. The indwelling Spirit is more powerful than any habit or pressure. Leave disciples with hope, not a heavier burden.

Purpose, Calling & Career

Discovering God's unique design for your life and integrating faith with vocation
"For we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand that we should walk in them." — Ephesians 2:10 NKJV

Young people around the world face enormous pressure around employment and life direction. This module speaks prophetically and practically: your calling is broader than a job title, your gifts matter to God and society, and the Kingdom needs transformed people in every sector.

Making Disciples — Becoming a Disciple Maker

The move from being discipled to discipling others
"And the things that you have heard from me among many witnesses, commit these to faithful men who will be able to teach others also." — 2 Timothy 2:2 NKJV

This is the capstone module of the entire curriculum. It takes everything learned and turns the disciple outward — to begin pouring into someone else. Disciple-making is simply living life intentionally with another person and pointing them toward Jesus.

The Disciple-Making Process — How Jesus Did It
1
Modelling — "I do, you watch"
Live openly in front of the person you are investing in. This requires vulnerability, not performance.
2
Assisting — "I do, you help"
Involve the disciple in ministry — co-leading, assisting, participating under supervision.
3
Watching — "You do, I watch"
The disciple leads; the mentor observes and gives feedback. The debrief is as important as the deployment.
4
Leaving — "You do, I am available"
The disciple leads independently; the mentor remains available for counsel.
5
Multiplying — "You do, with someone else"
2 Timothy 2:2 contains four generations in one verse: Paul → Timothy → reliable people → others.
Learning Objectives
  • Articulate the difference between evangelism and discipleship
  • Identify one person they will begin discipling within 30 days of completing the curriculum
  • Create a written 3-month discipleship plan for that person
  • Demonstrate ability to lead a basic Bible study and accountability conversation

Key Activities

  • Practice Session: Each disciple leads a 30-minute practice discipleship session with a partner. Feedback given by the group and mentor.
  • Disciple-Making Plan: Written plan submitted to the mentor — includes name, meeting schedule, topics, and accountability measures.
  • Passing the Baton: Completing disciples are paired with newer members as their first disciple.
