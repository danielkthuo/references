📖   Leader Notes

Welcome to Module 1 of this Discipleship Curriculum. This session explores the foundation of all Christian leadership and Kingdom living: Godly character. It is not about performance; it is about formation. Who we are becoming in Christ is more important than what we accomplish for Christ.

Lead with vulnerability. Share something real from your own journey. Many young adults carry a gap between their public Christian identity and their private interior life. This session should feel like a safe space to close that gap — not a performance review.

Session Overview

Duration: 75 – 90 minutes (weekly)

Audience: Young adults aged 18–25

Setting: Church classroom or youth hall

Materials: Bibles (NIV or ESV), handout, pens, whiteboard

Open with 2–3 min of reflective worship or surrender prayer.

Part 1

What Kingdom Character Really Means

From performance to transformation

"Therefore, as the elect of God, holy and beloved, put on tender mercies, kindness, humility, meekness, longsuffering."

— Colossians 3:12 (NKJV)

There is a version of Christianity that is mostly about behaviors — showing up to gatherings, using the right words, avoiding the obvious sins.

And there is a version of Christianity that is about transformation — actually becoming different, from the inside out, into the image of Jesus Christ.

The Kingdom of God is not built on performance. It is built on people. Specifically, on people whose character has been shaped by the Holy Spirit so thoroughly that when they lead, serve, decide, or suffer — what comes out of them is Christ.

Colossians 3:12 uses the word clothe — not perform, not pretend, not display. To clothe yourself with compassion means to put it on so that it becomes your covering.

Character Versus Performance

In recent decades, even in church culture, leadership has been reduced to skill, charisma, and strategy. We celebrate the dynamic speaker, the visionary planner, the networker. These are not wrong. But they can exist without character. And when they do, the result is leaders who build impressive platforms on unstable foundations.

Researcher and author Stephen Covey discovered that before 1940, almost everything written about leadership focused on character and values. After that, the emphasis shifted to techniques and appearances. The results, across institutions, organizations, and sadly across churches, have been deeply damaging.

God is not impressed by our strategy. He looks at the heart.

"For the LORD does not see as man sees; for man looks at the outward appearance, but the LORD looks at the heart."

— 1 Samuel 16:7 (NKJV)

Godly character includes:

IntegrityTruthfulness in all situations
HumilityA teachable spirit
Self-controlDiscipline over impulses
FaithfulnessConsistency in responsibility
PurityA clean moral life
AccountabilityWillingness to be corrected
CompassionKindness toward others
Illustration: The Respected Elder

Across many cultures, the most respected elders and community leaders are not those who are loud or self-promoting. They are those whose wisdom has been proven over time through consistent, honorable conduct.

Their authority is not based on position alone, but on a long history of integrity and reliability. People trust them before they even speak.

This reflects a deeper truth found in both culture and Scripture: leadership is earned through character, not claimed through titles.

Kingdom Truth: Inside-Out Leadership

Godly character works from the inside out. It is who we really are when we are alone and only God is watching. Our actions, decisions, and words eventually reveal our character — not the other way around.

You cannot consistently act better than you are. But you can allow God to make you better than you have been.

Competence vs. Character

Many young people today are developing strong abilities — academic excellence, leadership skills, communication skills, technical and digital skills, and professional competence. These are important and necessary.

However, a critical truth must be understood:

Competency gaps can be trained. Character failures are far more damaging and harder to repair.

Skills & Competence

Improved through teaching and practice

Can open doors

Can be demonstrated quickly

Measurable and trainable

Character

Formed through discipline, accountability, spiritual growth

Determines whether doors remain open

Built over years of small decisions

The foundation that holds everything else

A highly skilled person without character can still break trust, damage relationships, misuse influence, destroy credibility, and hurt others deeply.

A leader who lacks skill may frustrate people. But a leader who lacks character wounds people.

Biblical Example: Heart Over Ability

  • Saul had ability, but his character failed in obedience and humility.
  • David was not perfect, but he remained responsive to God — repentant and teachable.

God's selection principle remains the same: He looks at the heart.

Part 2

Sincerity — The Courage to Be Real

Without wax, nothing hidden

Without Wax

Imagine craftsmen at work in a marketplace, selling beautiful wooden carvings. The most skilled vendors sometimes display their work in shade, because shade hides the truth. Cracks and blemishes in the wood are filled with wax and polish, making a flawed piece appear perfect. Only the full sunlight reveals what is actually there.

The English word sincere comes from the Latin sin cere — meaning 'without wax.' To be sincere is to be a person who has nothing hidden, nothing filled in with performance or pretense.

To Be Sincere

To be without wax — nothing hidden, nothing covered up to create a false appearance.

In any community — especially a young adult community where image, social media, and comparison are constant pressures — sincerity is a radical act. It takes courage to let people see your actual self rather than the version you have curated.

"Let love be without hypocrisy. Abhor what is evil. Cling to what is good."

— Romans 12:9 (NKJV)

Sincerity in Scripture is not perfection. It is honesty before God and people — a life not built on image management.

"But the wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, willing to yield, full of mercy and good fruits, without partiality and without hypocrisy."

— James 3:17 (NKJV)

David and Peter: God's Standard

King David

David made serious moral failures, including adultery and murder. Yet Scripture describes him as a man after God's heart because he was honest before God (Acts 13:22). He repented openly, expressed brokenness in the Psalms, and returned to God sincerely.

His strength was not perfection — it was honesty before God.

Peter

Peter denied Jesus three times at a critical moment. Yet Jesus restored him and entrusted him with leadership in the early church. Peter was impulsive and imperfect, but he was real. Jesus did not choose a polished follower —

He chose a sincere one.

Devotional Pause — 2 minutes of silent reflection

Before continuing, take two minutes of quiet.

Ask yourself honestly: Where am I filling cracks with wax — presenting a version of myself to my community, or to God, that is not completely true?

You do not have to share this. But you do have to answer it.

Character failure is often not sudden — it begins with hidden cracks being covered instead of addressed.

Sincerity means:

  • Not pretending to be more spiritual than you are
  • Not hiding struggles behind performance
  • Not building identity on image
  • Not filling internal cracks with external polish

God is not calling young people to become perfect performers. He is calling them to become sincere disciples — real, honest, and transformed from the inside out.

Part 3

The Fruit of the Spirit

A character framework for Kingdom living

"But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control. Against such there is no law."

— Galatians 5:22-23 (NKJV)

Notice the image: fruit. Not a certificate. Not a spiritual achievement. Fruit grows over time, in the right conditions, from the right root. A fruit tree does not decide to produce fruit through effort — it produces fruit because of what it is rooted in.

As young adults in the Kingdom, our task is not to manufacture good character through willpower. It is to stay rooted in Christ — through Scripture, prayer, worship, community, and obedience — so that the fruit of His Spirit grows naturally out of us.

Fruit What it looks like in daily life Where it is tested in your world
Love Choosing the wellbeing of another over your own comfort, reputation, or convenience In how you treat a friend who has disappointed you
Joy A deep, stable gladness rooted in who God is rather than circumstances On the morning after a failure or disappointment that shook your faith
Peace Being a calm, grounded presence in tense situations In your home, workplace, or ministry when pressure and conflict rise
Patience Holding your response while trusting God's timing When God seems silent; when growth feels frustratingly slow
Kindness Intentionally helping or caring for others, especially unnoticed people Toward the difficult person, the outsider, or someone very different from you
Goodness Moral integrity in private choices, conversations, and habits When you could compromise or cut corners without any consequences
Faithfulness Consistently doing what you committed to do over time In your responsibilities to community, studies, work, and relationships
Gentleness Strength under control — correcting or leading without crushing others When someone under your influence makes a mistake
Self-Control Governing appetites, emotions, speech, and desires Your habits, anger, purity, and reactions when nobody is watching

Servant Leadership: The Kingdom Way

On the night before His crucifixion, Jesus — knowing He was Lord of all creation — knelt on the floor and washed the feet of twelve men, one of whom would betray Him within hours. Then He said:

"For I have given you an example, that you should do as I have done to you."

— John 13:15 (NKJV)

In Kingdom culture, the greatest is the servant of all. Position is not the reward for character — it is the responsibility that character equips you to carry.

Illustration: The Worship Leader Who Swept the Floor

A young worship leader at a growing church was widely praised for her voice and her anointing. One Saturday before service, the building attendant was sick and the hall was dusty. She was the first to arrive.

Without announcing it, she swept the entire hall, set out the chairs, and arranged the communion table. By the time the congregation arrived, no one knew she had done it.

Years later, when the senior pastor retired and the board sought a successor, her name was the first raised — not because of her singing, but because of how she was known to behave when no one was watching.

Character is always witnessed. It just takes time.

Part 4

Forming Character — The Daily Walk

No shortcuts. No programs. Only ordinary faithfulness.

"He who says he abides in Him ought himself also to walk just as He walked."

— 1 John 2:6 (NKJV)

There are no shortcuts to Godly character. There is no prayer that instantly produces it, no conference that downloads it, no program that manufactures it. Character is formed in the ordinary — in the thousand small decisions that no one records or rewards.

The Apostle Paul calls us to a 'renewing of the mind' (Romans 12:2) — a deep restructuring of how we think, what we value, and how we instinctively respond. This happens through consistent, humble, daily engagement with God and community.

Three Disciplines That Form Character

1

Daily Immersion in Scripture

Character is formed when God's Word consistently reshapes the way we think, respond, and live. This is the daily practice of allowing Scripture to renew your mind and reveal the kind of person God is forming you to become.

"What does the life of Jesus teach me about the person I am becoming?"

Observe how Jesus treated difficult people. How He handled pressure. How He responded to criticism. How He used power. And how He remained faithful in suffering. The goal is not only to know Scripture — the goal is to become more like Christ.

"And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind."

— Romans 12:2 (NKJV)
2

Accountable Community

You cannot see your own blind spots. Every person has them: unhealthy habits, pride, emotional reactions, hidden compromise, or patterns they cannot recognize alone. That is why Scripture calls believers into honest, close community.

"But exhort one another daily, while it is called 'Today,' lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin."

— Hebrews 3:13 (NKJV)

Biblical accountability is not control or criticism. It is choosing relationships where truth, encouragement, correction, and growth are welcomed. Identify two or three trusted believers — mentors or mature friends — who have permission to ask honest questions.

Character grows best where honesty is safe.

3

Examined Prayer

At the end of each day, bring your actual self before God — not the polished version. Examined prayer is the practice of reflecting honestly before God:

  • Where did I reflect Christ today?
  • Where did I fail?
  • What attitudes shaped my actions?
  • What needs repentance or growth?

"Let the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart be acceptable in Your sight, O LORD, my strength and my Redeemer."

— Psalm 19:14 (NKJV)

This is not about guilt or self-condemnation. It is about remaining honest before God so transformation can continue. Growth begins where honesty begins.

Warning: The Titanic's Rivets

When the Titanic was finally examined decades after sinking, analysts discovered that the three million rivets holding its hull together had been filled with inferior materials rather than pure steel. On the surface, the ship was flawless. Underneath, it lacked integrity.

When the iceberg came, it was not the steel hull that gave way — it was the rivets. The plates separated. The ship sank.

Many Christian leaders present a strong exterior: serving, speaking, praising. But the interior rivets — the private character — are compromised. When the iceberg comes (and it will), what holds?

The good news: God is in the business of replacing inferior rivets. That is called sanctification. But it requires your honesty and cooperation.

Activity

Small Group Discussion

Groups of 3–4 · 12–15 minutes

Instructions

Break into groups of 3–4. Spend 12–15 minutes working through these questions together. Choose a timekeeper and someone to share one insight with the full group at the end.

  1. Fruit of the Spirit Reflection Which fruit of the Spirit seems most naturally present in your life right now? Which one feels weakest or most absent? What experiences, habits, or influences may have shaped each?
  2. Character That Impacted You Describe a moment when someone's character — not their talent, title, or skill — deeply impacted you. What specifically about them earned your respect or trust?
  3. Filling Cracks with Wax Where in your life right now are you at greatest risk of 'filling cracks with wax' — performing character you have not yet actually formed?
  4. Examined Prayer What would change in the next 30 days if you took seriously the discipline of examined prayer every evening?
Personal Reflection

Between You and God

Instructions

Take 8–10 minutes in quiet. These questions are between you and God. Write honestly.

  1. Your Private Self Who are you when nobody else is watching? In what ways does your private life differ from your public image?
  2. Inferior Rivets What 'inferior rivets' currently exist in your inner life — hidden areas of compromise, weakness, inconsistency, or unresolved struggle?
  3. An Honest Conversation with Jesus Imagine Jesus sitting with you right now, speaking about your character with perfect love and truth. What areas do you think He would encourage, correct, heal, or invite you to grow in?
  4. One Practical Step What is one specific step you will take this week to allow God to form your character rather than managing your image?

Closing Declaration — Read Aloud Together

"I choose to be known not by my performance, but by my character. I will allow the Holy Spirit to form in me what I cannot manufacture myself. I commit to honesty before God, authenticity in community, and consistency in private. I am a disciple in formation — not yet finished, but fully surrendered."

"Let this mind be in you which was also in Christ Jesus."

— Philippians 2:5 (NKJV)
